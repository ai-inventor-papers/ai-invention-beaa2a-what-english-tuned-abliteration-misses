## Leg C: downstream sensitivity (a range, not a re-estimate)

11. Yoon et al. (arXiv 2608.22490, Sec. 3) state that "we instantiate Munalg using a safety-ablated (abliterated) version of the models". Assume per-language safety cost is an aligned-minus-unaligned difference in refusal-rate units; this functional form is an ASSUMPTION, not established by the verified material. Under it, an ablated counterfactual verified with an English keyword score would misstate the SL-minus-EN cost difference by between -1.68 and -0.11 refusal-rate units (negative = Slovene residual refusal under-reported relative to English) across the three ablated checkpoints and all reference channels measured here. Measured by the reference, the ablated checkpoints themselves leave an SL-minus-EN residual refusal gap of [-0.05, +0.78]. The two-language ordering of residual refusal inverts between the keyword view and the reference view: True. This is a sensitivity statement for one model pair plus one outside-family community edit, under NF4, with machine-translated Slovene (native review PENDING) and two languages. It is not a re-estimate of any published number and not a claim that any published ranking is wrong. [source: results/downstream_sensitivity.csv]
12. Lineage: the principle that an English automatic proxy under-reports non-English damage comes from quantisation evaluation (Marchisio et al., arXiv 2407.03211: "a 1.7% average drop in Japanese across automatic tasks corresponds to a 16.0% drop reported by human evaluators on realistic prompts"). This artifact extends it to refusal scoring; it does not originate it. [source: results/downstream_sensitivity.json]
