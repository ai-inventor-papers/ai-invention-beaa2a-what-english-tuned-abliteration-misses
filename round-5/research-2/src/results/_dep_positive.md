## Paste-ready paragraph

Refusal in chat models is mediated by a one-dimensional subspace that can be removed by orthogonalising every matrix that writes to the residual stream, and the canonical method applies that removal "at all layers and all token positions" (Arditi et al., 2024, §3.1) [OBSERVATION]. That default makes the placement question invisible: if the edit covers the whole stack, where in depth it acts is not a free parameter [INTERPRETATION; the alternative reading is that placement is simply irrelevant once the direction is right, which is what our matched contrasts test]. Two lines of work have since made placement a question again. Selecting intervention sites by measured causal effect rather than by probe quality is now established in activation space — probe-best and steer-best layers dissociate, with the steer-best layer confined to normalized depth [0.48, 0.74] while the probe-best layer scatters across [0.29, 1.00] across three omni-modal architectures (arXiv 2609.22135, Fig. 1) — and the same separation has been shown in text language models, where "the layer with the highest probe accuracy is not the layer with the largest ablation response, in every pretrained model we ran" (arXiv 2606.00926) [OBSERVATION]. In weight space, the parallel statement is older and blunter: edit success is "essentially unrelated to where factual information is stored in models, as measured by Causal Tracing" (Hase et al., 2023, §1) [OBSERVATION]. For refusal specifically, a 24-model abliteration study reports that uniformly spread layer selection "substantially outperform[s] norm-based selection (targeting layers with the highest refusal signal norm) by up to ∼70pp of additional refusal reduction" (arXiv 2607.02714, §3.2), and a transplantation study localises refusal to mid-network MLP blocks with a non-additive composition in which "adding more aligned blocks can reduce refusal performance" (arXiv 2608.11583, abstract) [OBSERVATION]. On the language side, the refusal direction itself is close to universal — "a vector extracted from English can bypass refusals in other languages with near-perfect effectiveness" across 14 languages (Wang et al., 2025, abstract) — while safety-sensitive *layers* are not: "sensitive layers are only partially shared across languages", with peaks at layers 12–14 for Japanese and Korean and 18–19 and 29 for Dutch (arXiv 2609.22144) [OBSERVATION]. **What this work adds is the consequence of that asymmetry for a persistent weight edit of bounded size: holding total removal energy AND the number of edited layers fixed, which contiguous band carries the energy decides how much refusal survives, and it decides it differently per language and differently between two sibling checkpoints.** In `cjvt/GaMS3-12B-Instruct`, twelve contiguous mid-depth layers leave Slovene residual refusal at 0.24 while twelve layers strided over the full depth at the same total energy leave 0.81 (difference -0.57 [-0.70, -0.44]; `round-3/experiment-10/src/results/report_tables.md`, group E3), the pre-registered broad-beats-narrow prediction is falsified in the opposite direction (-0.10 [-0.150, -0.060], same file), and a nested decomposition puts log-energy alone at R² 0.325, log-energy plus where the energy sits at 0.718, and log-energy plus how many layers at 0.400 (same file) [OBSERVATION]. In `google/gemma-3-12b-it`, the lowest reachable Slovene refusal is monotone in the fraction of layers 13–24 covered (Spearman -0.942, label-permutation p = 0.0038; `round-3/experiment-9/src/results/report_tables.md`), and sparing layers 25–36 costs Slovene +0.39 residual refusal against 0.00 in English (same file) [OBSERVATION]. **Where it stops:** two sibling checkpoints of one architecture plus one community reference and one outside family; one non-English language in the matched panels (two further languages only in the outside-family arm); one or two optimiser seeds, so no run-to-run variance claim is made and prompt-level intervals are never presented as optimiser variance; 4-bit NF4 throughout with a single bf16 control cell; Slovene material machine-translated with automated back-translation QC only, native review PENDING (`results/stopping_points.md`) [OBSERVATION]. Two checkpoints are two units, not a population: nothing here is attributed to Slovene continual pretraining, to instruction tuning, or to any safety-training stage, because no training stage was varied [OBSERVATION].
