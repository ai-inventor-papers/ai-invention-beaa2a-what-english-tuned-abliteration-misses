The finding

A refusal counter that cannot read Slovene

removes refusal from a language model by orthogonalising its weights against a extracted from English contrast pairs. The optimiser, , decides which candidate edit to keep by counting English refusal phrases in the model's answers.

The question: when that certifies an edited checkpoint, does it tell you anything about refusal in Slovene?

The answer is no. On 100 answered by the Gemma edit, the counter fires on 87% of English responses and on 0% of Slovene responses. The finds 13% English refusal and 82% Slovene refusal. The counter over-fires in the language it was written for and is silent in the other.

    How it works

    One prompt pair through the pipeline

    The stepper follows one real pair from the verified set through Heretic's search, its selection rule, the edited Gemma model's answers and the two instruments that score them. The pair is picked by a fixed rule: among pairs from a non-violent harm category (fraud, defamation, specialised advice, privacy, intellectual property, elections, code abuse) whose English row is the original RefusEU prompt, both languages refused by Gemma-orig, the edit's English answer flagged by the counter but not judged a refusal, and its Slovene answer judged a refusal that the counter misses, take the one with the shortest English prompt. Use the buttons, the numbered markers or the left and right arrow keys.

      The failures, one click away:

      What the paper does not show

      • The Slovene judge is not certified. The Qwen3-14B workhorse judge reaches unweighted = 0.72 [0.63, 0.81] against GPT-4.1 on Slovene, below the 0.80 gate. English passes at κ = 0.86. Absolute Slovene rates carry this caveat; within-Slovene comparisons are less affected. The depth-placement experiment's judge also missed its gate (κ = 0.683).
      • Answers are truncated. 94–95% of edited English outputs hit the 256-token limit, so the judge often classified refusal from incomplete text. The item browser tags each capped answer.
      • Two models, one architecture family. The paper observes the GaMS3 and Gemma divergence but does not attribute it to continual pretraining, the training mix or any other factor.
      • Placement does not beat dose. The write-profile overlap orders cells within a model and replicates in Qwen3-8B, but the primary criterion was falsified (ΔR2 0.027 and 0.056), and the frozen depth index failed cross-model prediction (ρ = −0.009 over 21 pooled cells).
      • One language, one precision, one search per model. Slovene only; all checkpoints in 4-bit ; one Heretic configuration (116 trials) per model.
