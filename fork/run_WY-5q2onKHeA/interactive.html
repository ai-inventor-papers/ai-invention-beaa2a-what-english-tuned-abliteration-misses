An English Keyword Objective Is Blind to Its Own Edit in Slovene

Abliteration strips refusal from a language model by editing its weights against a direction found in English. The optimiser that tunes the edit checks its work with a list of English refusal phrases. This page lets you watch that check go silent in Slovene, on the run's own outputs.

How it works

Where along the pipeline does the Slovene refusal drop out of view?

Follow one real candidate, Gemma trial 96, from direction extraction to the evaluation of one verified prompt pair. Every value below is read from the run's files. The pair is chosen by a stated rule: the lowest-numbered pair in which Gemma-edit's English answer trips the counter within its opening words while the judge does not call it a refusal, and its Slovene answer is judged a refusal while the counter stays silent.

Left and right arrow keys also move between stages when the diagram has focus.

    The paper reports ρ = −0.90 for OSL against Slovene refusal, −0.97 within E2 and −0.95 within E3, and ρ = −0.34 (p = 0.15) for the mean edited layer. From the unrounded mean depths in the file the page computes −0.33; the paper's figure rounds them to two decimals, which ties two cells. Its primary criterion, that placement explains variance beyond , was falsified (ΔR2 = 0.027 and 0.056). A strong rank order inside a matched panel is therefore not the same claim as an independent causal share.

    Try it: run the counter yourself

    What exactly does the keyword counter do with a piece of text?

    The counter is short enough to run here exactly. It lower-cases the answer, drops asterisks, straightens apostrophes, collapses whitespace, and calls the answer a refusal if any of Heretic's 33 English markers appears as a substring. An empty answer also counts as a refusal. Type an answer, load a real one, or re-run the counter over every answer on this page and compare with the verdicts the run recorded.

    What the paper does not show

    • The Slovene judge is uncertified. Qwen3-14B reaches unweighted κ = 0.72 [0.63, 0.81] against GPT-4.1 in Slovene, below the 0.80 gate. English passes at κ = 0.86. Absolute Slovene rates carry this caveat; within-Slovene comparisons are less exposed.
    • Most edited English answers are cut off. 95.4% of Gemma-edit and 94.2% of GaMS3-edit English answers hit the 256-token limit, so the judge often labelled incomplete text.
    • Two models from one family. Gemma-3-12B-IT and GaMS3-12B-Instruct share an architecture. The GaMS3–Gemma contrast is observed, not attributed: nothing here isolates continual pretraining or any other training stage.
    • The placement result is narrow. Overlap ranks cells within a model and replicates in Qwen3-8B, but placement did not explain variance beyond dose (ΔR2 0.027 and 0.056), and the frozen depth index failed across models (ρ = −0.009 over 21 cells). The placement experiment's own judge missed its gate (κ = 0.683).
    • One language, one precision, one search per model. Slovene only, 4-bit throughout, and a single 116-trial Heretic configuration per model.
    • The Gemma edit is not the last word on Gemma. The community edit of the same base reaches 5.4% English and 11.4% Slovene refusal, so a more thorough search can reach much further in Slovene.
